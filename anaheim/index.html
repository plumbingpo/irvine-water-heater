How Anaheim Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
