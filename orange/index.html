Keeping Your Orange Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms such as rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone today so help is one call away as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Hot Water Headaches Orange Homeowners Face

Every type of water heater fails in its own way, and we work on all of them around Orange. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units burn out elements and thermostats, especially when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor trouble, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they struggle in cramped closets.
