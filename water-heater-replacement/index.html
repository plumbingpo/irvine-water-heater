Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Replacement?

Swapping a water heater looks simple until you count the connections. Gas units need a leak-free connection with a sediment trap, a correctly sized and pitched vent that sends carbon monoxide outdoors, and enough combustion air. Electric units need a breaker and circuit that match the element wattage. Every tank needs a correctly rated relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

Professional replacement gets all of it right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your household, verify the gas line, vent, and electrical service, and install whatever code items apply. Before leaving, we leak-test, verify draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and set a safe temperature. That is the difference between a heater that runs quietly for a decade and one that starts with a problem on day one. And if a repair would buy you several good years on your current tank, we tell you that honestly instead of selling you a new one.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
